# Design principles

Source: [Apple HIG — Design principles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles),
reintroduced 8 June 2026. These are tools for weighing priorities, not
a single recipe.

## Purpose

Create value. Orient every decision toward what makes the product
genuinely useful. At every stage ask what the product is for and
whether the design serves that purpose.

Keep focused. Prioritize the features people actually use and make
those great. A product with a clear use helps people meet their goals.

Find new ways to solve the problem. Study existing solutions without
recreating them. Define what sets the product apart and let the design
reflect that.

## Agency

Stay out of the way. People use the product to get something done. Get
them to the task or content. The best designs are unobtrusive and
present when needed.

Give people freedom to explore. Do not lock them into flows or modes.
When a guided flow is necessary, make it easy to skip or escape.

Help people recover from mistakes. Reversible actions make exploration
safe. Forgiveness must be easy. Recovery must not cost their work.

## Responsibility

Be transparent about what the product does and why. Intentions are
clear from the first interaction. Permission asks include a rationale.
If data is gathered, say what and why.

Keep people’s information safe. Collect only what the product needs.
Anticipate misuse and put protections in place.

## Familiarity

Use concepts people already know from the real world and from other
software.

Keep visuals and interactions consistent. Once a behavior or look is
established, keep it. Consistency teaches and builds confidence.

Provide clear feedback. Show when controls are available, when content
changes, and use system patterns for alerts and choices.

## Flexibility

Design for everyone. Treat accessibility as a starting requirement, not
a polish pass.

Preserve a person’s context. Keep content and controls in predictable
places as the design adapts. Use natural animation for transitions.

Consider many inputs: pointer, keyboard, VoiceOver, and later voice.
Approach every platform with intention — Bronze is a Mac app and must
feel like one.

## Simplicity

Include just what is necessary. Simplicity is not minimalism. Keep
important things close; let the rest fall away.

Be concise. The simplest wording is usually the most universal.

Establish hierarchy. Form and function must be apparent so people know
where they are and what comes next.

## Craft

Quality sets the tone. Be deliberate: type, motion, wording, spacing.
Prototype, discard what fails, test in real use. Shipping is not the
finish line.

## Delight

Identify the emotion the product should inspire. Bronze should feel
calm, precise, and trustworthy — a work queue, not a toy.

Create defining moments at real interactions (a successful copy, a
failed capture), not ornamental chrome.

Do not mistake delight for decoration. People are trying to finish a
task. Practical restraint is the aesthetic.

Delight is the sum: freedom to act, safety to explore, familiar
metaphors, and flexible context. It is not extra shadow, extra
tracking, or extra animation.
